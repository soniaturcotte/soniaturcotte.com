## April 

### The Grapes of Wrath by John Steinbeck
Incredibly written if deeply depressing. I’m glad I started my Steinbeck journey with East of Eden, because this didn’t really take you on the up journey, just the down. But what a journey into the depths it is. In this house, we stan Steinbeck.

### Conflict Is Not Abuse: Overstating Harm, Community Responsibility, and the Duty of Repair by Sarah Schulman
This book properly challenged me. Of course, we read to learn and most things give you a little push but this was a Big Old Shove. Incredible.

### Giovanni’s Room by James Baldwin
Crushing. Like pressing your finger on a bruise. Even if one must look aside from the way he writes about women and trans people in this book.

### House of Day, House of Night by Olga Tokarczuk
I read Flights years ago with my book club, and found it difficult. I like narrative. Drive your Plow is one of my favourite novels of all time. This one was more like Flights, but I think I appreciate form and pure craft more now that I’m a baby writer and so I did like this. I did like the way it shows that borders are stupid and made up. Anything that’s made up can be unmade.

### Alchemised by SenLinYu
A little fantasy escapism. I had no idea about the whole Manacled lore until I got to the end and then I felt bad for the last line of the book not f-ing me up the way it apparently did to everyone online.

### Interrogating the Global Social Challenges, ed. Kevin Gillan, Vanessa May
An edited collection with chapters by tons of people in my department, including both my supervisors. Entry-level introductions to various themes. I read to get to know the department better.

### Livestock/Deadstock: Working with Farm Animals from Birth to Slaughter by Rhoda M. Wilkie
Only one animals book this month! What have I been doing? Oh yes, I got my ethics approval. Research starting. If you know a farmer, introduce me :)
 

## May  

### Life Beside Itself: Imagining Care in the Canadian Arctic by Lisa Stevenson
This is a masterclass in ethnographic writing. Beautiful, heart-wrending, quietly groundbreaking. It has nothing to do with my PhD research but sometimes you just gotta read things that inspire. From her research integrity to writing craft, 10 out of 10.

### Governing Bodies: A Memoir, A Confluence, A Watershed by Sangamithra Iyer
There is lots to recommend about this book; it’s reflective and meandering and often poetic. I think the personal-essay-slash-memoir is one of my favourite genres. However, Iyer does this moral superiority thing that I find quite grating. The bits on family and belonging are lovely.

### The Covenant of Water by Abraham Verghese
Generational epic set in South India. Rich and compelling characters and a very interesting premise with the water thing (no spoilers). I confess, I was more drawn to the early characters in the book (Ammachi) and a little dissatisfied with the resolution of the water thing… a little too ‘hymn to progress’ for my taste. But it’s a fantastic narrative and if you are willing to take on a ~750pager (or 31hr audiobook, in my case) then I do recommend.




## June 

### Anti-Oedipus: Capitalism and Schizophrenia by Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari
What a book! I loved reading this so much, despite (or maybe because) of how much of a head-fuck it is. I listened to 10 lectures on the first chapter alone, and for a fleeting moment thought I understood a Body Without Organs.

### Sula by Toni Morrison
Morrison will pull out your heart, grate it up into little slivers, mix in as much devastation as possible, and then shove it back in your chest cavity to fester. I remember this feeling from when I read Beloved from start to finish in one sitting on a redeye transatlantic. Some of us like this feeling.

### Forest of Noise: Poems by Mosab Abu Toha
Poems about life in Gaza, from the bombs to moments with family. Painful and raw and beautiful. A free Palestine in our lifetime, inshallah.

### Project Hail Mary by Andy Weir
The movie was sweet and cute, the book much the same if a little less saccharine and a little more ’science’. It’s not on the nerd level of The Three Body Problem but good if you like a little math with your entertainment. The flaws of the main character more central in the book.

### Bury Our Bones in the Midnight Soil by V.E. Schwab
Sapphic vampire brainrot but make it stressful.

### The Party by Tessa Hadley

### Qualitative Researching by Jennifer Mason
One can, in fact, read the word “epistemology” too many times. This is one of the better methods handbooks though.
