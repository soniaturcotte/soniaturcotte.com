In [*The Sounds of Life*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691206288/the-sounds-of-life), Karen Bakker<label for="sn-bakker-ref" class="margin-toggle sidenote-number"></label><input type="checkbox" id="sn-bakker-ref" class="margin-toggle"/><span class="sidenote">Bakker, Karen. 2024. The Sounds of Life. Princeton University Press.</span> tells tales of turtles speaking to each other. Scientific researchers long believed that turtles don’t make sounds, but a few intrepid PhD researchers set out to prove otherwise. 

I love this story for many reasons, not least because it exemplifies the impoverished view human supremacy gives the world. These turtles (Podocnemis expansa) are amazing. For example, received scientific wisdom was that turtles abandon their eggs after laying them. Not only is this not true, mother turtles wait in the waters off the beach for their hatchlings and make specific noises to call to them. But also, incredibly, the baby turtles make noise before they hatch. Bakker writes, 

> Researchers have yet to provide a difinitive answer, but the most likely answer is that the yet-to-be-hatched turtles are sharing their readiness to break out of their eggs, in order to coordinate a time to hatch ... synchronizing their departure from the nest makes each individual hatchling less vulnerable to predators. <span class="small-text">(p72-73)</span>


One of the reasons it took so long to recognise turtle communication is because they don’t vocalise loudly, their sounds are subtle and quiet. 

> ...they leave relatively long pauses between one sound and the next, and might wait minutes or even hours before replying to one another. <span class="small-text">(pg 66)</span>

I also think about these turtles a lot as a researcher for two reasons. First, on an epistemological scale, to always remain open to surprise. How little we know of the world and of each other. Second, methodologically, on how to be a polite interlocutor. Imagine a conversation where you wait 10, 15, 20 seconds, even a minute, before responding? Can we try to really listen to those with whom we speak?
